Where is the electron found?

Lithium has three electrons: two in the small 1s orbital (gold) and one in the larger 2s orbital (teal). Each curve is drawn to its own height. The 2s curve drops to zero at its node, the dotted sphere where its electron is never found. The shaded area is the chosen share of one electron. Each shell is a boundary, not a path: the electron is found somewhere inside it. One a₀ is about 0.053 nm. These shapes are approximate (Slater's rules).

About lithium

  • The lightest metal, and the least dense solid element (about 0.534 g/cm³), so light that it floats on water.
  • Its atomic mass is about 6.94 u. The most common form (about 92.5%) has three protons, four neutrons and three electrons.
  • A soft, silvery-white metal that can be cut with a knife. It tarnishes quickly in air and is usually stored in oil.
  • Melts at about 180.5 °C, the highest melting point of the alkali metals.
  • Placed in group 1 (the alkali metals) because it has one outer electron, which it loses easily. The two 1s electrons sit close to the nucleus and hold on tightly.
  • Widely used in rechargeable batteries.
Distances are in Bohr radii (a₀), about 0.053 nm each.
