Reading the picture

Sodium has eleven electrons: two in 1s (gold), two in 2s (teal), six in 2p (violet) and one in 3s (coral). The first ten fill the neon core, so the eleventh must start a new shell.

The 3s orbital is far larger than everything inside it. The ten inner electrons shield most of the +11 charge, so this electron feels a pull of only about 2.2. That is why sodium gives it away so easily.

Each shape holds 99% of one electron. It is a boundary, not a path. The dotted spheres mark places where the electron is never found.

About sodium

  • A soft, silvery metal you can cut with a knife. It tarnishes in seconds in air.
  • Atomic mass about 22.990 u. Sodium-23 is its only stable isotope.
  • Group 1, an alkali metal. Its lone 3s electron is lost easily, forming Na⁺ with neon's arrangement.
  • It reacts fiercely with water, giving hydrogen gas and sodium hydroxide.
  • Table salt is sodium chloride, NaCl. Sodium street lamps glow yellow-orange.
