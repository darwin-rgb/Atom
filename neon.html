Where is the electron found?

Each curve shows how likely the electron is to be found in a thin spherical layer at that distance, so it tells you how far, not which direction. Each curve is drawn to its own height. The 2s curve drops to zero at its node, then rises again. The 2p curve has no such dip: it climbs smoothly to a single peak, and it stays away from the nucleus more than the 2s does, because an orbital with a plane of zero probability through the nucleus has almost no electron very close to the centre. The shaded area is the chosen share of one electron. All the 2p orbitals have the same shape, so one 2p curve stands for each, whether it holds one electron or two. For 2p that share counts every direction, so the distance it gives is a little different from the lobe reach above.

Reading the picture

Neon has ten electrons: two in the small 1s orbital (gold), two in the larger 2s orbital (teal), and six in 2p (violet). The sixth 2p electron fills the last empty space, so all three 2p orbitals now hold two electrons each, with opposite spins. That completes the second shell. Every orbital is full, so neon has no unpaired electron to share and no gap to fill. Its nucleus pulls very hard on the 2p electrons (an effective charge of 5.85), so they are held tighter than in any other atom of this row. Because the three 2p orbitals are filled equally, their combined electron density depends only on the distance from the nucleus. So the whole atom is round, even though each single orbital has two lobes. Try Cloud view with All three at 99% to see the dots form a ball. Each 2p orbital is two lobes with a flat plane between them where the electron is never found. Choose a single 2p to see that plane as a faint violet disc. The 2s orbital has a dotted sphere where it is never found. Each shell is the smallest region that holds the chosen share of one electron. It is a boundary, not a path. One a₀ is about 0.053 nm. These shapes are approximate (Slater's rules).

About neon

  • A colourless, odourless noble gas. It is very rare in air, about 0.002% by volume. Liquid neon boils at about −246 °C.
  • Its atomic mass is about 20.180 u. It has three stable isotopes: neon-20 (by far the most common), neon-21 and neon-22.
  • Placed in group 18, with a full outer shell of eight electrons (2s² 2p⁶). Under ordinary conditions it forms no stable compounds.
  • It has the highest first ionization energy of any element in this row, because a strong nucleus holds a full, symmetrical shell.
  • Fluorine becomes the fluoride ion, F⁻, by taking one electron. That gives it exactly neon's arrangement of electrons.
  • When electricity passes through neon gas, it glows red-orange. That is the light of the classic neon sign.
Distances are in Bohr radii (a₀), about 0.053 nm each.
